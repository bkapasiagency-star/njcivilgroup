Why the dig matters

The cheapest part of your build is the dearest one to stuff up.

Everything else stacks on top of the excavation. When it's late, over-dug or out of position, you wear it in concrete, in trades standing around and in weeks you can't get back. Five things go wrong on Sydney sites. Here's how we shut each one down.

01

The operator doesn't show

What it costs youPump, concreter and steel all pushed back. One lost day turns into a lost fortnight.

We lock a date and turn up on it

If the weather moves it, you hear from us first, with a new date. No radio silence.

02

Over-dug footings

What it costs youEvery extra 50 mm of depth is concrete you pay for and never see again.

Dug to the drawings, not by eye

Levels checked as we go. Trenches kept clean and square so the pour matches the quote.

03

Piers in the wrong spot

What it costs youFailed inspection, engineer back out, a redrill and a slab date that slides.

Set out off your surveyor's pegs

Every pier on the engineer's grid, down to depth, ready for the inspector to tick off.

04

Spoil left sitting on site

What it costs youNo room for the next trade, mud on the street and a neighbour on the phone to council.

Dug and carted in the one go

Trucks are booked with the machine, so the dirt leaves the same day it comes out.

05

Tight block, close neighbours

What it costs youCracked driveways, flattened fences and an insurance argument nobody wins.

Right size machine for the block

Careful operators, fully insured, and boundaries treated like they're our own.

How it runs

Keep scrolling. Watch your block get dug.

Ring us or send your plans and you get a fixed price in writing. Then this is what happens on your site.

1

Strip and set out

Grass, old slabs and rubbish gone. The block is scraped back to clean ground and the pad is strung out off your surveyor's pegs.

2

Pier to the grid

Every pier drilled where the engineer drew it and down to depth. Holes cleaned out, ready for the inspector.

3

Cut the footings

Edge beams and internal beams cut square and to level. Spoil goes straight in the truck, not in a pile on your nature strip.

4

Hand it over, ready to pour

We walk it with you or your builder before we leave. Steel goes in, the pump turns up and pour day stays pour day.
